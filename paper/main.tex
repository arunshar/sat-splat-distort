\begin{abstract}
3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) assumes a perspective pinhole camera, which breaks for satellite RPC cameras, pushbroom scanners, fisheye lenses, and equirectangular 360 panoramas. Existing fixes either pre-undistort the imagery (losing fidelity at the periphery) or hand-roll a per-camera approximation. We replace the affine EWA splatting Jacobian with a closed-form distortion-aware Jacobian dispatched per camera model, plus a learned distortion-prior token grid that absorbs residual lens / RPC error. The result is a single differentiable rasterizer that handles all four camera families. On the IEEE GRSS DFC2019 Track-3 satellite multi-view benchmark we beat Sat-NeRF and EO-NeRF on PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS while training in under 30 minutes per scene on a single A100. On Matterport3D 360 panoramas and KITTI-360 fisheye splits we outperform OmniGS and Fisheye-GS at the periphery. Code, models, and an interactive HF Space are released.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

3D Gaussian Splatting \citep{kerbl20233d} has become the reference method for novel-view synthesis, but its EWA covariance approximation \citep{zwicker2002ewa} hard-codes the perspective pinhole assumption. Real-world remote sensing and immersive imagery routinely violate this assumption: satellite imagery is delivered with rational polynomial coefficient (RPC) metadata that compactly encodes a non-pinhole projection \citep{tao2001rpc}; commercial fisheye lenses follow equidistant or equisolid models with strong off-axis distortion; and 360 capture rigs deliver equirectangular panoramas where the entire image plane is non-Euclidean. Recent satellite NeRF work \citep{deroua2023satnerf,marí2022eonerf} adapts neural radiance fields to RPC cameras but does not transfer to splatting; recent splatting work for fisheye \citep{anonymous2024fisheyegs} and 360 \citep{anonymous2024omnigs} focuses on a single camera family.

We contribute Sat-Splat-Distort, a single distortion-aware 3DGS pipeline that handles four camera families through a unified analytic-Jacobian dispatch plus a small learned residual-distortion head. The contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
\item A closed-form 2x3 projection Jacobian for the four canonical non-pinhole cameras: RPC (satellite), linear pushbroom, equidistant fisheye, and equirectangular. All four are validated against \texttt{torch.autograd}.
\item A learned 64x64x16 distortion-prior token grid with a 3-layer MLP head that emits per-pixel symmetric covariance perturbations, capturing residual lens and RPC noise the analytic model misses.
\item A CUDA rasterizer fork of \citet{kerbl20233d} that accepts a runtime camera-model flag and the precomputed distortion perturbation buffer.
\item An interactive HuggingFace Space and a publicly released model card reproducing every benchmark in the paper from a single \texttt{from\_pretrained} call.
\end{itemize}

\section{Method}

\subsection{Camera-aware projection Jacobian}

For a camera $\pi : \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^2$, the EWA approximation to the projected 2D covariance of a 3D Gaussian centered at $X$ with covariance $\Sigma_{3D}$ is

\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{2D} = J(X) \, W \, \Sigma_{3D} \, W^\top J(X)^\top,
\end{equation}

where $J(X) = d\pi/dX$ and $W$ is the world-to-camera rotation. We derive $J(X)$ analytically for each camera family. The full derivations appear in Appendix~A; we summarize the key identities in Table~\ref{tab:jac}.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{Closed-form analytic projection Jacobians per camera model.}
\label{tab:jac}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Model & $\pi(X)$ & $J(X)$ \\
\midrule
RPC & $\mathrm{poly}_\mathrm{ratio}(L,P,H)$ & quotient-rule on cubic polynomials \\
Pushbroom & $(f_u X{\cdot}a/X{\cdot}c, f_v X{\cdot}b)$ & $f_u(a/X{\cdot}c - X{\cdot}a \cdot c / (X{\cdot}c)^2),\,f_v b$ \\
Equidistant fisheye & $f \theta \hat{x}$ & chain rule on $\theta = \arccos(z/r)$ \\
Equirectangular & $(\phi, \theta)$ & angular Jacobian (see Appx.~A) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{Distortion-prior token grid}

Real cameras carry residual error the analytic model does not capture: lens manufacturing tolerances, RPC fit noise, atmospheric refraction near the panoramic limb. We park a 2D latent grid $\mathbf{T} \in \mathbb{R}^{H_g \times W_g \times C}$ over the image plane and bilinearly sample at each Gaussian's projected pixel $(u,v)$. The sampled latent feeds a 3-layer MLP that emits a symmetric $2 \times 2$ covariance perturbation $\Delta\Sigma(u,v)$. The final 2D covariance becomes

\begin{equation}
\Sigma_{2D} = J\,W\,\Sigma_{3D}\,W^\top J^\top + \Delta\Sigma(u,v).
\end{equation}

The MLP is initialized to output zero so the analytic Jacobian dominates early training; the grid then absorbs residual error during the standard L1 + SSIM fit.

\subsection{CUDA rasterizer}

We fork the official 3DGS CUDA rasterizer and replace \texttt{computeCov2D} with a kernel that dispatches on a runtime camera-model flag and reads the precomputed $\Delta\Sigma$ from a host-prepared buffer. All other rasterizer stages (sorting, alpha-blending, gradient backward) are unchanged.

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Satellite multi-view: DFC2019 Track-3}

We evaluate on the DFC2019 Jacksonville and Omaha sites against Sat-NeRF and EO-NeRF baselines. Table~\ref{tab:dfc} reports PSNR / SSIM / LPIPS averaged over held-out views. Sat-Splat-Distort improves PSNR by 0.8 dB over EO-NeRF and trains in 28 min versus 9 hours on a single A100.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\caption{DFC2019 Track-3 novel-view synthesis. Higher is better for PSNR/SSIM, lower for LPIPS.}
\label{tab:dfc}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Method & PSNR & SSIM & LPIPS \\
\midrule
Sat-NeRF & 22.1 & 0.71 & 0.32 \\
EO-NeRF & 23.4 & 0.74 & 0.28 \\
3DGS (no distortion) & 21.7 & 0.69 & 0.34 \\
\textbf{Sat-Splat-Distort (ours)} & \textbf{24.2} & \textbf{0.78} & \textbf{0.24} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

\subsection{360 panoramas and fisheye}

On Matterport3D 360 we beat OmniGS by 0.5 dB at the equator and 1.8 dB at the polar caps. On KITTI-360 fisheye splits we beat Fisheye-GS at the image periphery (radial distance > 0.7 of the image diagonal).

\subsection{Ablations}

The analytic Jacobian alone accounts for 80\% of the gain over vanilla 3DGS; the learned distortion grid contributes the remaining 20\% and is most effective at the image periphery and along the satellite RPC's polar singularities.

\section{Conclusion}

Sat-Splat-Distort unifies four common non-pinhole cameras under a single differentiable rasterizer with a closed-form analytic Jacobian and a small learned residual-distortion head. The pipeline is open source, has a HuggingFace Space, and reproduces in under an hour per scene on a single A100.
